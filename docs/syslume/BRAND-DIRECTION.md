## Research and interpretation

The [Central Karakoram landscape](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6112/) provides a geographic reference for contours, changing scale, and exploration. This is an artistic connection to navigating system data, not a claim that a generated scene depicts a particular real valley.

The [Aga Khan Trust for Culture's Shigar publication](https://the.akdn/en/resources-media/resources/publications/aga-khan-trust-culture-shigar-fort-residence-baltistan) describes Balti woodcarving preserved at the fort. Its craft context informs precise joinery and framed openings; the concepts do not reproduce a documented historic carving.

[UNESCO's Chitral heritage work](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/preserving-heritage-and-empowering-livelihoods-chitral) supplies context for living craft traditions. The [Pakistan National Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://heritage.pakistan.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/ICH%20Pakistan%20Low.pdf) describes Suri Jagek as Kalasha observation of celestial bodies in relation to the landscape. The horizon concept is our own metaphor for attention and measurement; it is not a representation of a ceremonial symbol or endorsement by that community.

## Application design

Use forest `#426B42`, sage `#AED09B`, ivory `#EEF2E6`, charcoal `#151B17`, and brass `#DBC27C` as the final palette. Pink, blue and red are excluded from the application design. Local Windows typography, tabular numbers, clear labels and accessible chart explanations support the monitoring views. Warning states use brass and written descriptions.

The opening screen should answer “How is my computer doing?” and provide six obvious destinations. Advanced values are revealed on demand. Brand imagery serves welcome and explanation; live readings retain most of the space.
